## Why dedicated finance tools matter

Indian retail finance is not a single product. A home loan from SBI, a cooperative bank FD, and a post-office RD each use different compounding conventions, tenure options and tax treatment. Generic overseas calculators often assume monthly compounding only, ignore processing fees, or skip HRA's three-part exemption rule entirely.

MerQPrime tools are tuned for India: rupee formatting, annual rates with quarterly FD compounding, RD maturity via the standard instalment formula, and HRA limits at 50% or 40% of basic salary depending on city type. Every tool shares the same design — large inputs, sticky result panels on desktop, copy/share actions — so once you learn one calculator, the rest feel familiar.

Privacy matters for finance. Salary, loan amounts and rent figures are sensitive. These tools never require an account and do not send your numbers to a backend for calculation. That makes them safe for quick checks on shared computers or during client calls.

## Loan planning: EMI and total borrowing cost

Most Indians encounter EMIs through home loans, car finance, personal loans or education credit. The [EMI Calculator](/tools/emi-calculator) applies the reducing-balance formula lenders use: monthly instalment, total interest and a month-by-month amortization table. Change tenure or rate and see how a longer loan lowers EMI but raises lifetime interest — a trade-off every borrower should visualize before signing.

The [Loan Calculator](/tools/loan-calculator) extends the same math with an optional processing fee (flat or percentage). NBFCs and banks often quote a low rate but charge upfront fees that increase true cost. Adding the fee to your scenario gives a honest comparison when two offers look similar on paper.

Read our guide [How to Calculate EMI](/blog/how-to-calculate-emi) for the formula, worked examples and tips on comparing offers. If you are also building investments alongside debt, cross-check with the [Investment Tools](/investment-tools) hub for SIP and PPF projections.

## Deposits: FD and RD for disciplined saving

Fixed deposits suit lump sums you can lock away; recurring deposits suit monthly saving habits. The [FD Calculator](/tools/fd-calculator) models maturity with quarterly, monthly or annual compounding — matching how most Indian banks quote FD returns. The [RD Calculator](/tools/rd-calculator) uses the standard maturity formula for equal monthly instalments.

Deposits are not automatically the best return in every period — equity SIPs carry higher risk — but they remain the default for risk-averse savers and emergency buffers. Our [FD Calculator Guide](/blog/fd-calculator-guide) and [RD Calculator Guide](/blog/rd-calculator-guide) walk through formulas, tax on interest and when each product fits.

Senior citizens often get higher FD rates; enter the applicable rate directly. TDS on interest applies above threshold limits — factor tax into your real return when comparing to PPF or debt funds on the investment hub.

## Salary and tax: HRA exemption

House Rent Allowance is one of the most common salary components for urban employees. Exempt HRA is the minimum of three figures: actual HRA received, rent paid minus 10% of basic salary, and 50% of basic (metro) or 40% (non-metro). The [HRA Calculator](/tools/hra-calculator) applies that rule instantly and shows which limit binds your exemption.

Use results when reconciling Form 16, negotiating salary structure, or deciding whether paying rent to family (with legitimate receipts) qualifies. HRA is separate from Section 80C deductions — PPF and ELSS live under different limits, covered in our [PPF Calculator Guide](/blog/ppf-calculator-guide) on the investment hub.
