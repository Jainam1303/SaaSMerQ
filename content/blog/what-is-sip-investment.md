A Systematic Investment Plan (SIP) lets you invest a fixed amount in a mutual fund at regular intervals — usually monthly. It is one of the most popular ways Indians build long-term wealth without timing the market.

## How SIP works

Each month you invest a set amount (for example ₹10,000). Units of the mutual fund are purchased at the fund’s net asset value (NAV) on the purchase date. When markets fall, you buy more units; when markets rise, you buy fewer. This is called rupee cost averaging.

## Why investors choose SIP

- **Discipline** — investing automatically builds a habit.
- **Accessibility** — start with small amounts.
- **Compounding** — returns earned on past returns grow over decades.
- **Flexibility** — increase, pause or stop SIPs per fund rules.

## SIP is not a separate product

SIP is a **mode of investing** into a mutual fund scheme, not a fund category itself. You can run SIPs in equity, debt, hybrid and index funds depending on your goals and risk tolerance.

## Estimating SIP returns

Future value depends on:

- Monthly investment amount
- Expected annual return (a planning assumption, not a guarantee)
- Investment duration

A common planning formula assumes monthly investments at the start of each period:

**FV ≈ P × [((1+r)^n − 1) / r] × (1+r)**

Where P is monthly investment, r is monthly return rate, and n is number of months.

## SIP vs lump sum

| Approach | Best when |
| --- | --- |
| SIP | Regular income, reducing timing risk, long horizons |
| Lump sum | Large one-time capital, strong conviction on entry point |

Many investors combine both.

## Risks to understand

- Mutual fund returns are **not guaranteed**.
- Equity SIPs can be volatile in the short term.
- Past performance does not predict future results.
- Consider expense ratios, taxes and exit loads.

## Getting started

1. Define your goal (retirement, education, house down payment).
2. Pick fund category aligned with horizon and risk.
3. Set a monthly amount you can sustain.
4. Review annually — do not panic on short-term dips.
