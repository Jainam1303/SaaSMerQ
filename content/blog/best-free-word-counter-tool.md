## Why word count still appears in content briefs

Google has stated word count is not a direct ranking factor. Comprehensive content that satisfies search intent often ends up longer because it covers subtopics, examples and FAQs readers expect. Briefs use word count as a **proxy for depth**, not a magic threshold.

Use counts to:

- Match client or editorial minimums consistently.
- Compare draft revisions (did the rewrite add substance or fluff?).
- Trim meta descriptions and titles to typical SERP display limits.
- Audit competitor pages during research (paste public excerpts only).

Blindly padding words hurts quality. Pair counting with outline coverage — every H2 should answer a real question.

## What a good free word counter should measure

### Words

Most tools split on whitespace. Edge cases include hyphenated compounds, numbers, URLs and CJK characters — algorithms vary slightly. For SEO briefs, small differences rarely matter; consistency within one tool does.

### Characters and characters without spaces

Meta descriptions are character-limited. Twitter/X and LinkedIn previews also truncate by character width. MerQPrime shows both totals so you can hit "155 characters" targets from [Meta Tags for SEO](/blog/meta-tags-for-seo) without manual spreadsheet counting.

### Sentences and paragraphs

Structural counts help readability reviews. Very long paragraphs may hurt mobile scanning; sentence averages hint at complexity.

### Reading time (optional)

Some counters estimate minutes at 200–250 WPM. Useful for blog headers ("5 min read") when the estimate matches your audience.

## Word count for SEO metadata

### Title tags

Aim for concise, unique titles — often cited roughly 50–60 characters before ellipsis in SERPs. Count characters in the [Word Counter](/tools/word-counter), then generate tags with the [Meta Tag Generator](/tools/meta-tag-generator).

### Meta descriptions

Typical guidance: ~150–160 characters for desktop snippets; mobile may truncate differently. Write compelling copy first, then trim using character counts. Read [Meta Tags for SEO](/blog/meta-tags-for-seo) for duplication and rewrite behavior when Google ignores your description.

### Open Graph text

Social platforms may show longer titles than Google. Generate OG tags with the [Open Graph Generator](/tools/open-graph-generator) and verify preview text length separately from SERP meta — two audiences, two optimizations.

### URL slugs

Slugs are not word-counted the same way — length in characters and word count both matter for readability. Use the [Slug Generator](/tools/slug-generator) after headline lock; keep slugs short and keyword-clear.

## Word count for body content

### Pillar pages and guides

Long-form guides (like this one) target thorough coverage. Count words after outline completion to find thin sections — add examples, tables or FAQs rather than adjectives.

### Product and landing pages

Conversion pages may be shorter if every line sells. Count supports consistency across locales when translating — compare English and Hindi draft lengths to spot missing sections.

### Blog vs documentation

Docs prioritize clarity over length; blogs may compete on depth. Use the [SEO Tools](/seo-tools) hub to align article schema ([What Is Schema Markup](/blog/what-is-schema-markup)) with actual on-page FAQ content — word count should reflect real answers.

## Common mistakes when using word counters

### Counting HTML markup

Paste visible text only, or strip tags first. Counting `<div>` classes inflates totals misleadingly.

### Including boilerplate

Headers, footers and legal disclaimers repeated on every page skew comparisons — isolate main content when benchmarking competitors from view-source snippets ethically.

### Chasing density

Keyword stuffing triggered penalties in old SEO lore; modern systems penalize unhelpful content. Count words; do not force keyword percentages.

### Ignoring readability

Flesch scores appear in some tools; MerQPrime focuses on core metrics. Read aloud — awkward sentences fail users regardless of count.

## Word count for multilingual and Indian English content

Indian audiences consume English and regional language content. Character counts differ for Devanagari or Tamil scripts — character-based limits still apply on social previews. Test rendering on devices your users carry.

Mixed-language pages (Hinglish marketing) should count sections separately if translators hand off partial drafts.
